# External mathematical inputs and their precise scope

The external-basis policy remains unrestricted. Classical foundational results are used externally; the geometric reductions, response comparisons, exact finite algebra, and central nonconvex-exclusion theorem are proved within the project. The separate convex-pentagon extremal theorem appears only as the explicit premise of the conditional appendix corollary.

The detailed original-text checks reported below are records inherited from the preceding Chinese analytic edition. This English revision preserves those records and checks their role against the current proof. It does not claim to have reread every linked original work during this revision. The historical labels E1, E2, E3a, and E3b refer to the input classification in the supplied proof.

Analytic perturbation from Kato is thus confined to the finite-parameter Steiner argument. The separate citation to Kato's closed-form representation theorem is retained because it underlies the definition of the comparison operator.

## Inputs no longer needed for the response estimates

The crossing proof uses a positive singular comparison function, the cotangent estimate, and two quotient-curvature identities. In R3 that cotangent estimate is proved by an elementary Sturm comparison using both zero endpoints. It removes all inverse-third trace constructions and the characteristic-coefficient identity. Mercer's theorem and the Green-kernel trace calculation are therefore no longer external premises of the current proof. The real-order comparison operator is defined for every \(2\le s\le3\); its common Hardy form domain, compact resolvent, interface matching, and identification of regular solutions with minimizing extensions are proved explicitly.

R4 gives H.6 a direct whole-interval comparison. One positive function on the singular piece is glued to a positive free polynomial, and the derivative jump is included in the quadratic-form identity. The residual is bounded and positive, and the common Hardy form domain and compact attainment justify the strict spectral conclusion. H.6 therefore no longer uses the exact regular solutions, adjacent-order recurrence, separated-spectrum estimates, or the cotangent interface response. Its only direct semantic dependency is the comparison operator definition; H.5 retains its own regular-solution and cotangent inputs. This change introduces no new external theorem.

The cotangent partial-fraction expansion and the Basel reciprocal-square sum are no longer used in the response proof. R.7 now derives the same rational lower bound from an explicit smooth positive comparison function whose logarithmic derivative is exactly the prescribed response. An explicitly decreasing cubic determines one sign change of its differential residual. The Wronskian with the sine function increases and then decreases between its two zero endpoint values, proving the strict inequality. The preceding finite projection, resolvent, and complementary spectral-gap argument are no longer needed for R.7.

R.6 constructs regular inverse-square solutions by a Volterra integral equation and a global contraction in an equivalent norm. The proof supplies the convergence, uniqueness, endpoint asymptotics, and variational interpretation directly. First-order differential factorization proves the exact adjacent-order identity. One positive comparison function then gives the original algebraic tail response with an explicit nonnegative differential remainder. The regular-solution power series and coefficient comparison are no longer needed in R.6 or the crossing proof. Other analytic constructions, including the corner estimates in the geometric foundation, retain their stated classical basis.

The estimate \(1-x\cot x>x^2/3\) on \((0,\pi)\) now follows from the two derivative identities for \(J(x)=\sin x-x\cos x\) and \(F(x)=(1-x^2/3)\sin x-x\cos x\). The rational bounds for \(\pi^2\) use finite polynomial division and signed integral remainders for \(\arctan\), together with elementary tangent identities; an infinite alternating-series truncation is not required.

Simple-polygon triangulation, the Jordan theorem, winding-number homotopy invariance, elementary compactness, and standard one-dimensional trace and Sobolev facts remain classical inputs. Their uses respect the stated polygonal simplicity, finite triangulation, fixed container, or form-domain hypotheses. Supplementary computation does not serve as a substitute for these results.
